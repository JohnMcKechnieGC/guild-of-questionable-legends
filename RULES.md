Gather **2–4 players**, exactly one of whom is Ledger, the computer. The other 1–3 adventurers share a screen. Seats run clockwise in the order shown.

1. Shuffle all **36 cards** and deal them face down, one at a time clockwise, into equal piles: 18, 12 or 9 per player. Choose a dealer at random. The next seat clockwise chooses first.
2. Pass the screen to a human chooser before revealing their top card. Until a stat is called, only the chooser's top card is visible. Choose **Weapon Skill, Strength, Toughness, Luck, Charisma or Intelligence**. Each stat uses the same 1–100 scale; higher always wins.
3. Reveal every active player's top card and compare the called stat. A single highest score wins every played card, including the winner's own, plus the pot. Put the new cards at the bottom in fixed seat order, starting at seat 1, then add the old pot oldest first. The winner chooses next.
4. If the highest score is shared, put **every played card** into the pot in that same seat order. Keep the stat locked. **Every player with cards**, including anyone who did not share the highest score, plays their next card. Repeat until one score is highest. That winner takes the new cards and the entire pot.
5. Empty piles are eliminated. If the original chooser runs out during a tie, keep comparing the locked stat. If just one player has cards after a tied comparison, they take the pot and win.
6. **House rule — all-empty draw:** if everyone runs out on the same tied comparison, leave all the cards in the pot, declare a draw and offer a rematch. Otherwise, the player holding all 36 cards wins.

Ledger chooses by uniformly sampling **three distinct stats** from its own top card and selecting the highest of those three. It breaks equal scores randomly, never examines opponents' cards to choose, and never chooses again while a stat is locked.

Read each **round result**, then press **Continue**. A comparison only advances when you press its action button. Passing, choosing and resolving a tie are separate steps.

There is no turn limit or artificial sudden death. Some deals can take a long time or cycle; you can leave a match and start a new deal from the tavern.
